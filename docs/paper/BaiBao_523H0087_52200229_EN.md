# An Evidence-Grounded Framework for Similarity Analysis and Rubric-Based Assessment of Student Reports

**Nguyen Tran Ngoc Quy and Vo Thi Kim Yen**

Faculty of Information Technology, Ton Duc Thang University, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

## Abstract

Evidence-grounded assessment requires traceable retrieval and explicit failure analysis. We propose a two-pipeline framework combining similarity analysis with an authored-checklist rubric-assessment prototype. Our empirical study evaluates PAN-PC-11 source retrieval and an auxiliary QASPER paragraph-retrieval task; rubric-grading effectiveness remains unvalidated. The positive-only, oracle-span DEV0 diagnostic contains 30 parent annotations, 320 correlated query windows, and 200 candidate sources. Parent-balanced Recall@10 is 0.8594 for BM25, 0.8219 for multilingual E5, 0.8300 for reciprocal rank fusion, 0.6597 for Dense plus Cross-Encoder, and 0.7558 for fusion plus Cross-Encoder. Paired parent-cluster bootstrap deltas for adding reranking are -0.1622 [95% CI: -0.2848, -0.0476] for Dense and -0.0742 [-0.1855, 0.0396] for fusion. Candidate-flow diagnostics identify hard gating, support-passage mismatch, and truncation in 97.5% of scored pairs as plausible failure mechanisms without isolating causality. A prespecified short-input comparison on disjoint DEV1 data eliminates additional model truncation but changes Recall@10 from 0.7729 to 0.7384 (difference -0.0346; paired 95% CI: -0.1028, 0.0369), providing no evidence of improvement. Separately, within-paper retrieval on 646 evaluable QASPER development questions yields Hit@10 of 0.7693, 0.8669, and 0.8715 for BM25, E5, and fusion. E5 has higher complete-evidence retrieval than fusion (0.7430 versus 0.7245). This auxiliary experiment uses shared retrievers without rubric verification or scoring. These are development-set component results, not official PAN scores or end-to-end grading validation; the untouched PAN TEST0 holdout remains unevaluated.

**Keywords:** evidence-grounded assessment; similarity analysis; source retrieval; BM25; dense retrieval; reciprocal rank fusion; Cross-Encoder; rubric assessment

## 1 Introduction

The growing volume of digitally submitted student reports creates an opportunity to reduce repetitive grading work, but a defensible assessment still requires more than a score. An instructor needs to know which passage triggered a similarity finding, which source was retrieved, which rubric requirement was checked, and which evidence supports or contradicts the proposed outcome. A system that cannot preserve these links may appear accurate while remaining difficult to audit, correct, or use responsibly in a consequential educational setting.

Similarity analysis and plagiarism judgment are also different tasks. A system may identify lexical or semantic overlap, but legitimate quotation, conventional terminology, shared task descriptions, and correctly attributed paraphrases can all produce similarity. Conversely, paraphrasing may reduce surface overlap while preserving borrowed content. PAN-PC-11 and the associated PAN evaluation campaigns formalized source retrieval and text-alignment tasks for this problem [7, 8], while newer resources such as PlagBench examine LLM-generated and LLM-detected plagiarism [11]. The appropriate system output is therefore a set of traceable candidate sources and aligned evidence, not an automatic accusation.

Rubric-based assessment introduces a related transparency problem. A predicted score alone does not show whether a criterion failed because evidence contradicted a claim, evidence was absent, or the system could not retrieve enough context. Rationale-aware scoring methods such as RMTS [16] and academic-assessment frameworks such as RubiSCoT [17] make progress toward explainable feedback. Long-document and multi-hop benchmarks further show that relevant evidence may be distributed across pages or documents [12--15, 18]. However, these component advances do not by themselves define a provenance-preserving workflow that connects document parsing, retrieval, claim verification, deterministic rubric rules, and instructor review.

We address this gap with the two-pipeline framework summarized in Fig. 1. Pipeline 1 retrieves candidate sources for suspicious passages by combining lexical and semantic views, then retains source and passage provenance for review. Pipeline 2 compiles instructor-authored checklist definitions and routes each item to local passage retrieval or a structure-and-term graph baseline before applying explicit verification and scoring rules. Both pipelines are integrated into a working API, worker, database, and browser prototype. The empirical study is narrower than that implementation: it evaluates source retrieval and reranking in Pipeline 1 and reports an auxiliary within-paper evidence-retrieval experiment motivated by Pipeline 2. The latter uses shared retrieval algorithms rather than executing the rubric-assessment pipeline; neither experiment establishes rubric-grading effectiveness.

The primary PAN study asks three questions:

- **RQ1:** Under a locked, resource-bounded protocol, how do BM25, multilingual dense retrieval, and reciprocal rank fusion compare at early cutoffs and at candidate depth?
- **RQ2:** Does an off-the-shelf multilingual Cross-Encoder improve early source ranking over its input retriever?
- **RQ3:** Which candidate-flow and evidence-selection failure modes are observed alongside reranking gains or losses?

An auxiliary question examines how BM25, multilingual E5, and RRF recover human-annotated evidence paragraphs within a known research paper. QASPER [12] supplies the questions and evidence labels for this separate retrieval task; it does not supply rubric checklists or grading outcomes.

The current contributions are: (1) a provenance-preserving two-pipeline architecture that separates evidence retrieval from instructor judgment; (2) a documented A1--A5 source-retrieval ablation on a frozen PAN-PC-11 development diagnostic, including parent-cluster uncertainty; (3) a failure analysis connecting rank transitions, support-passage overlap, and token truncation; (4) a prespecified, controlled input-construction comparison on a disjoint DEV1 pack; (5) a reproducible comparison of the three shared retrieval algorithms on human-annotated QASPER evidence; and (6) an explicit account of what these development experiments do not yet establish. The paper does not report classification of verbatim, paraphrased, or summarized reuse; official PAN text alignment; end-to-end rubric scoring; or performance on private student reports.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Plagiarism Source Retrieval and Text Alignment

The PAN plagiarism-detection campaigns separate the task into stages. Source retrieval searches a collection for likely source documents, whereas text alignment identifies reused spans within a suspicious--source pair [8, 10]. PAN-PC-11 provides a large corpus for external plagiarism detection [7]. PAN13 defines text alignment over paired documents and evaluates precision, recall, granularity, and PlagDet [9, 10]. This separation informs our architecture: a source missed during retrieval cannot be recovered by a later aligner, while a retrieved source is not yet proof of plagiarism.

PlagBench extends the problem to LLM-generated reuse and evaluates generation and detection across models and domains [11]. It is relevant to downstream similarity-type classification, but it does not replace source-retrieval evaluation. In this work, PAN-PC-11 supplies the source-document retrieval results, while QASPER supplies a separate paragraph-level evidence-retrieval experiment. PAN13 and PlagBench remain mapped to future alignment and classification experiments, respectively.

### 2.2 Lexical, Dense, Fusion, and Reranking Models

BM25 remains a strong lexical baseline because it rewards query-term matches while normalizing term frequency and document length [1]. Dense bi-encoders instead map queries and passages into a common vector space, enabling approximate semantic matching with independent encodings [4]. We use multilingual E5 [3] because the thesis targets both Vietnamese and English, although the present PAN diagnostic is not an in-domain Vietnamese evaluation.

Lexical and dense scores are not naturally calibrated. Reciprocal rank fusion (RRF) combines rank positions rather than raw scores and has been shown to provide a robust, training-free fusion rule [2]. Cross-Encoder rerankers jointly encode a query and candidate passage and can improve ranking when applied to a small candidate set [5]. The checkpoint used here is trained within the multilingual mMARCO ecosystem [6]. Rather than assuming that this reranker transfers to plagiarism-source retrieval, we test that hypothesis explicitly and report a negative DEV0 result for the current configuration.

### 2.3 Evidence-Grounded Rubric Assessment

QASPER anchors information-seeking questions and answers in research-paper evidence [12], while HoVer requires evidence across multiple Wikipedia articles [13]. HippoRAG proposes graph-based retrieval for long-term memory and multi-hop association [14], and RAGChecker decomposes retrieval-augmented generation quality into fine-grained diagnostic dimensions [15]. These works motivate separate LOCAL and GRAPH evidence paths, but none directly validates rubric scoring for long student reports.

RMTS augments multi-trait essay scoring with generated rationales [16]. RubiSCoT applies AI support across academic assessment and retrieves instructor-provided expectation documents, providing the closest system-level comparison [17]. MMDocIR targets evidence retrieval in long multimodal documents [18], and HARMOGEN-R provides human and AI rubric evaluations for formative programming assessment [19, 20]. We map these resources to component tests rather than presenting them as substitutes for an in-domain end-to-end dataset. Our QASPER experiment measures paragraph evidence retrieval using the shared retrievers; independently annotated rubric-assessment effectiveness remains unevaluated.

## 3 Proposed Evidence-Grounded Framework

### 3.1 Design Principles and Provenance

The framework follows three rules, reflected in the components and review boundary in Fig. 1. First, each machine-generated finding must cite inspectable evidence. Second, an instructor retains authority over similarity findings, rubric outcomes, and feedback. Third, insufficient evidence produces abstention rather than a guessed conclusion.

Uploaded reports are represented as immutable document versions. Format-specific PDF and DOCX adapters emit typed document elements with section paths and locators. The implemented provenance-aware chunker uses stable content-derived identifiers rather than sequence numbers, splits long text and tables deterministically, and retains half-open raw character spans. When normalization changes the text, a normalized-character-to-raw-span map is stored. Consequently, a retrieval result can be resolved through the chain

`file -> document version -> parsed element -> chunk/index entry -> analysis run -> evidence span`.

The current prototype uses MongoDB to store authoritative metadata, document and rubric versions, queued jobs, analysis results, review decisions, and evidence references. Indexes are treated as derived artifacts rather than the source of record. A finding is therefore not justified by an opaque chunk identifier alone. It carries the document version, raw span, locator, model/configuration version, and analysis-run identifier needed to reproduce it. The PAN source-retrieval experiment reported below uses a separate offline runner; its historical configuration and results are not replaced by later changes to the application prototype.

### 3.2 Pipeline 1: Similarity Analysis

The implemented diagnostic path and its top-50 reranking gate are shown in Fig. 2. Let a suspicious report passage be the query q and let the candidate source collection contain document passages p. The lexical view uses BM25:

`BM25(q,p)=∑ₜ∈q IDF(t)·TF(t,p)` (1)

The length-normalized saturation factor is `TF(t,p)=f(t,p)(k₁+1)/[f(t,p)+k₁(1−b+b|p|/avgdl)]`, where f(t,p) is term frequency, |p| is passage length, and avgdl is the mean passage length. A1 locks `k₁=1.2` and `b=0.75`.

The dense view encodes `query: q` and `passage: p` with multilingual E5 and ranks passages by cosine similarity. Passage scores are aggregated to source-document candidates before cross-view fusion so that a source containing many chunks cannot occupy multiple document ranks. For a source document d with rank r_v(d) in retrieval view v, RRF assigns

`RRF(d) = ∑ᵥ 1 / (k + rᵥ(d)), with k = 60.` (2)

The locked diagnostic retains the top 100 source documents from each input view. RRF forms their union. Two variants then rerank the first 50 documents with a Cross-Encoder. Each source must be represented by a selected support passage, so reranking quality depends on both the model and the passage-selection policy. The unreranked tail is retained to preserve candidate-depth recall, with an explicit marker that those documents received no Cross-Encoder score.

**Algorithm 1: provenance-preserving candidate retrieval and reranking**

1. Parse and chunk every source while retaining stable IDs, source spans, and locators.
2. Retrieve top-100 source documents with fixed-window BM25 and/or structure-aware E5.
3. Aggregate passage scores to unique source documents.
4. For the hybrid variant, fuse source ranks with equal-weight RRF (`k=60`).
5. Select support passages and rerank the top 50 sources with the Cross-Encoder.
6. Preserve the remaining candidate tail; attach retrieval scores, rerank scores, support spans, model revisions, and run provenance.
7. Return ranked evidence for instructor review; do not convert similarity into a plagiarism verdict automatically.

Downstream text alignment, four-way similarity classification (verbatim, paraphrase, summary, or no significant similarity), and document-level aggregation are part of the intended pipeline but are outside the present experiment. This distinction prevents source Recall@K from being misreported as plagiarism-detection accuracy.

The application prototype additionally provides opt-in bidirectional natural-language inference proposals for compared sentence spans, separately from exact-match coverage. These proposals require human review and do not establish copying provenance. Their authored development diagnostics are outside the PAN empirical study; full four-way classification, including summary detection, remains unevaluated.

### 3.3 Pipeline 2: Evidence-Grounded Rubric Assessment

The instructor authors criteria, criterion weights, atomic checklist statements, evidence requirements, and optional contradiction patterns through a form or structured JSON. Within a checklist, all evidence requirements must be supported; alternative phrases within one requirement are joined by OR. The compiler validates and canonicalizes these definitions, supplies explicit defaults, and hashes the compiled representation. Publication freezes a rubric version, which is then pinned to each analysis run. The compiler does not automatically decompose free-form criteria or infer checklist importance.

The author also selects each checklist's retrieval mode. LOCAL items retrieve a compact set of passages through BM25 or the configured hybrid retriever. GRAPH items require at least two authored evidence requirements and use a structure-and-term graph with Personalized PageRank to collect related passages. Claim/entity extraction and semantic multi-hop verification remain future work; the graph baseline is not evidence that such reasoning has been implemented.

Verification uses two levels. A claim--evidence relation is first labelled **SUPPORTED**, **CONTRADICTED**, or **INSUFFICIENT**. The checklist is then assigned **MET**, **UNMET**, or **CANNOT_ASSESS**, as formalized in Table 1. The current V4 verifier uses authored phrase requirements and contradiction patterns within the report, with lexical context rules for negation, prospective work, pending work, tool availability, and hypothetical statements. It retains exact spans for inspection and abstains when counterclaim scope is unresolved. These rules do not establish semantic entailment. Cross-source verification is not implemented: under the intended policy, a report statement A and an external statement B would count as a contradiction only when the external source is approved for the purpose and both statements concern the same entity, time, unit, and condition.

The implementation also provides an opt-in local language-model verifier, separately from the V4 baseline. For short LOCAL reports within explicit character, byte, and chunk limits, it can supply all parsed chunks rather than only the highest-ranked passages; exceeding a limit retains the ranked Evidence Pack instead of silently cropping the report. Code partitions the supplied text into source units with fixed identifiers and ranges. In a one-call variant, the model proposes requirement relations and selects those identifiers. In a two-call variant, the first call selects candidate units without deciding outcomes; the second sees only the selected text and rubric requirements and returns relations and rationales. Code resolves the identifiers to exact quotations, checks version and span provenance, and computes outcomes and scores. Digest, prompt, schema, policy, and request/response hashes make the proposal traceable. Invalid responses or exhausted budgets withhold a conclusion. Selecting all parsed chunks does not establish that the original document is complete, and a mechanically valid citation does not establish that it supports the model's interpretation. These optional modes are prototype implementations, not rubric-grading experiments reported in this paper.

| Claim--evidence relation | Checklist outcome | Decision condition |
|---|---|---|
| SUPPORTED | MET | Approved evidence satisfies the atomic requirement. |
| CONTRADICTED | UNMET | Authoritative, context-matched evidence directly refutes the requirement. |
| INSUFFICIENT | CANNOT_ASSESS | Evidence is absent, ambiguous, incomparable, or not authoritative enough. |

After verification, a proposed score is computed from explicit rubric rules rather than requested directly from a language model. Any **CANNOT_ASSESS** checklist, including an optional item, withholds its criterion score and the total score; an assessable subtotal is diagnostic only. When all items in a criterion are assessable, a **CORE** or **REQUIRED** item labelled **UNMET** applies the configured score cap. Otherwise the criterion receives its weight multiplied by the proportion of **MET** items. Custom level-based score interpolation is not implemented. Pipeline 2 therefore has an executable prototype, but its grading quality, evidence correctness, and human agreement remain to be evaluated on an independent annotated corpus.

### 3.4 Human Review Boundary

The system exposes evidence, model/configuration versions, and unresolved decisions to the reviewer. Instructors may record acceptance or rejection of a run and append item-level labels, evidence selections, and rationales. Review records are stored separately from the machine output, preserving the original prediction. Because reviewers can see that prediction, these records are not independent gold annotations. Targeted oral questions may later be generated from unsupported claims or cross-section inconsistencies, but question generation is not implemented or evaluated in the reported experiment and is therefore not included as a current contribution.

## 4 Experimental Design

### 4.1 Dataset, Sampling, and Governance

We use the external-detection portion of PAN-PC-11 [7, 8]. The local archives were checksum-verified against provider MD5 values, and the project records a research-use basis, restricted redistribution, a retention policy, and an approver. A deterministic converter sampled positive annotations across corpus strata with seed 20260923. The resulting DEV0 pack contains 30 parent plagiarism annotations, 320 overlapping query windows, and 200 candidate source documents. Each query is an oracle window taken from an annotated suspicious span, bounded to 256 word tokens with stride 128. Gold relevance is the annotated source document; the original source span is retained for diagnostic overlap analysis.

This setup answers a component question: given a known suspicious passage, does the retriever recover its source? It does not test how suspicious passages are discovered in a full report. It also contains positive queries only and therefore cannot measure false-positive or no-source rejection. Multiple windows originating from the same parent annotation are correlated, which motivates parent-balanced aggregation.

### 4.2 Locked Ablations

Table 3 locks the five variants to the same DEV0 queries, candidate-source collection, qrels, and metric implementation.

| ID | Retrieval view | Fusion | Reranking |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Fixed-window BM25 | None | None |
| A2 | Structure-aware multilingual E5 | None | None |
| A3 | A1 + A2 | Equal-weight RRF (`k=60`) | None |
| A4 | A2 | None | Cross-Encoder over top 50 |
| A5 | A1 + A2 | Equal-weight RRF (`k=60`) | Cross-Encoder over top 50 |

A1 and A2 differ in both retrieval model and chunking view. Their comparison is therefore a system-view comparison, not a clean one-factor test of lexical versus semantic scoring. A3 tests whether equal-weight fusion improves candidate coverage or early ranking. A4 and A5 isolate the effect of adding the configured Cross-Encoder to A2 and A3, respectively. In A4, each source document is represented by the support passage selected by Dense retrieval. In A5, up to two distinct supports--one from fixed-window BM25 and one from structure-aware Dense retrieval--are scored, and the maximum raw Cross-Encoder logit becomes the source-document score. Only ranks 1--50 are reranked; documents at ranks 51--100 retain their upstream order and are appended as an unscored tail.

### 4.3 DEV0 Metrics

Recall@K records whether the gold source appears within the first K unique source documents. MRR@10 and nDCG@10 measure early rank quality. Query-window macro averages over 320 windows. Because one long annotation can generate many windows, the primary diagnostic is parent-balanced macro averaging: first average a metric over the windows of each parent annotation, then average over the 30 parents,

`Mparent=|P|⁻¹·∑ p∈P |Qₚ|⁻¹·∑ q∈Qₚ M(q)` (3)

Candidate Recall@100 is reported separately from early ranking. It indicates whether a later stage has access to the correct source, but it does not guarantee correct reranking or evidence alignment.

To avoid treating the 320 overlapping windows as independent observations, uncertainty is estimated with a paired parent-cluster bootstrap. Metrics are first averaged within each parent annotation; the 30 parents are then sampled with replacement 10,000 times using seed 20261012. Every method and paired contrast uses the same parent draw. We report type-7 percentile 95% confidence intervals as descriptive uncertainty on DEV0, not as confirmatory significance tests.

### 4.5 Prespecified DEV1 Input-Construction Comparison

A separate development comparison uses a regenerated DEV1 pack with 30 parent annotations from 30 suspicious documents, 207 correlated query windows, and 200 candidate source documents. The same checksum-verified converter was run from a clean commit with seed 20261012, excluding DEV0. DEV0 and DEV1 have no overlap in parent annotations, suspicious documents, or the complete candidate-source pool. Both retain the positive-only, oracle suspicious-span task; numerical differences across these two collections are not method-effect estimates.

The protocol and implementation were frozen at clean commit `2a8c073072ba41af8f05c79e75124aeef833f0d3` before inspecting DEV1 model outputs. A1--A5 retain their existing settings. The primary contrast is A6 minus A5: A6 retains A5's source candidates, pre-rerank order, top-50 gate, per-view support passage IDs, checkpoint, raw-logit scoring, maximum pooling, and preserved tail. It changes only the strings submitted within those fixed support requests. The query is preserved if it fits or reduced to a prefix of at most 192 model tokens. One contiguous passage window of at most 256 model tokens maximizes distinct whole-word matches with the visible query, resolving ties at the earliest position. Selection has no access to gold spans or relevance labels. The exact locked tokenizer verifies the cropped strings and the resulting pair; deterministic end trimming repairs boundary retokenization when needed.

Primary reporting uses parent-balanced Recall@10, with MRR@10, nDCG@10, candidate Recall@100, and actual tokenizer diagnostics as secondary outcomes. Paired parent-cluster intervals use 10,000 shared draws, seed 20261028, and type-7 percentile limits. The artifact audit checks identical A5/A6 candidates, supports, head/tail membership, and upstream ranks. Crop ranges and hashes describe the scored pair strings; full original raw spans are not automatically treated as model-visible evidence. Shared model and exact-pair cache reuse preclude interpreting sequential method times as independent cold-run latency.

This contrast evaluates a combined input-construction policy, including query-prefix trimming and lexical passage selection; it cannot isolate truncation alone. A separately regenerated TEST0 pack is disjoint from both development packs and remains sealed for a subsequent frozen evaluation.

### 4.6 Auxiliary Within-Paper Evidence Retrieval on QASPER

We evaluate the shared retrieval algorithms on QASPER v0.3 DEV [12], independently of the PAN source-retrieval protocol. The full split contains 281 papers and 1,005 questions. Each original question retrieves only abstract and full-text paragraphs from its own paper, producing 13,547 candidate paragraphs across the split. A candidate is a dataset paragraph, not an application parser chunk. The benchmark calls the shared BM25, Dense, and RRF utilities directly; it does not execute Pipeline 2's checklist-query builder, LOCAL orchestration, Cross-Encoder, verifier, or scoring rules. The task therefore tests within-paper evidence retrieval as a proxy motivated by the assessment design.

Human evidence strings are mapped to paragraphs after Unicode NFKC normalization, case folding, and whitespace normalization. An evidence string must identify exactly one paragraph in its paper, and all evidence in every answerable annotation must map completely. Questions with figure/table evidence, section-heading evidence, missing evidence, duplicate-paragraph ambiguity, mixed answerability, or only unanswerable annotations are excluded. These reasons can overlap. The resulting evaluation contains 646 questions from 255 papers; 359 of the original 1,005 questions are excluded. The same questions and candidate paragraphs are used for every method, and gold evidence is not appended to retrieval queries. Multiple complete human annotations define alternative acceptable evidence sets rather than one mandatory union.

BM25 uses `k1=1.2` and `b=0.75`. Dense retrieval uses the same E5-small revision, query/passage prefixes, CPU device, and batch size specified in Sect. 4.4. Both return Top-10 paragraph lists. Equal-weight RRF uses `k=60` to fuse these two lists and returns Top-10; no relevance reranker is applied. Results are question-macro averages at K = 1, 5, and 10. Hit@K requires at least one retrieved paragraph belonging to any acceptable annotation. Best recall@K is the maximum fraction of a human annotation's evidence set retrieved, and Complete@K requires all paragraphs of at least one acceptable annotation. MRR@10 uses the first paragraph belonging to any acceptable set. These protocol-specific metrics are not the official QASPER answer or evidence F1 scores.

The reported values come from a clean-code revalidation on 30 October 2026 at commit `9b9721d6b0d0eb9069b95b4d937635c2d9e3924e`. The input DEV JSON is checksum-locked, paper selection uses seed 20261018 and includes all 281 papers, and the manifest records source hashes, model revision, configuration, coverage, and environment. The complete metrics file is byte-identical to the earlier diagnostic, with SHA-256 `e254444c3921a6a189caf7888f62e127a471fcd94a0132e2dbe63d9da4bff237`. The historical RRF source hash differs, so this establishes aggregate revalidation of the frozen implementation rather than identical historical source or per-query rankings. The DEV split was previously inspected; no untouched QASPER holdout, confidence interval, or confirmatory superiority test is reported.

Relative to A2, A4 loses 22.19 percentage points of query-window Recall@10 and 16.22 points of parent-balanced Recall@10. Relative to A3, A5 loses 21.25 and 7.42 points, respectively. Recall@100 is unchanged within each reranked pair because the candidate tail is preserved. The observed loss is therefore in early ordering, not candidate availability at depth 100.

### 5.2 Parent-Cluster Uncertainty

The parent-cluster bootstrap quantifies how uncertain the DEV0 differences are. Parent-balanced Recall@10 is 0.8594 [95% CI: 0.7527, 0.9484] for A1, 0.8219 [0.7037, 0.9240] for A2, 0.8300 [0.7158, 0.9274] for A3, 0.6597 [0.5252, 0.7915] for A4, and 0.7558 [0.6253, 0.8718] for A5. Parent-cluster resampling shows substantial uncertainty with only 30 parents. The A3-A2 and A5-A3 intervals include zero for all three early-ranking metrics; neither the small RRF point gain nor the A5 point loss supports a confirmatory superiority claim on DEV0. ### 5.3 Fusion Transition Analysis

Fusion does not help every query uniformly. Table 8 isolates the relevant rank transitions. There are 36 windows for which Dense places the gold source in the top 10 but RRF does not. The median gold-source rank after fusion for this group is 57.5, outside the top-50 reranking gate. A5 can therefore rerank only four of these gold sources. In the opposite direction, RRF moves 22 Dense misses into the top 10. A5 retains 12 of these rescues.

| Transition group | Cases | Relevant observation |
|---|---:|---|
| Dense top-10 -> RRF outside top-10 | 36 | Median RRF gold rank 57.5; most are inaccessible to top-50 reranking |
| Dense outside top-10 -> RRF top-10 | 22 | Fusion supplies complementary lexical evidence |
| A2 miss -> A4 hit | 22 | Cross-Encoder can rescue some Dense errors |
| A2 hit -> A4 miss | 93 | Losses substantially exceed gains |
| A3 miss -> A5 hit | 13 | Some fused candidates benefit from reranking |
| A3 hit -> A5 miss | 81 | Net early-rank effect remains negative |

At query-window level, these transitions accompany higher Recall@100 but lower Recall@10 for RRF relative to Dense. At parent level, RRF changes Recall@10 by only +0.0081, with a paired 95% interval of [-0.0644, 0.0831], and changes Recall@100 from 0.9902 to 0.9854. Equal-weight RRF with one candidate quota is therefore not automatically optimal when the two views have different failure modes.

### 5.4 Cross-Encoder Failure Analysis

The reranker must score a query against one or more passages representing each candidate source. A4 passes the relevant source into the top-50 rerank stage for 300 query instances, but the selected support passage overlaps the annotated gold source span in only 199 cases (66.3%). In the remaining 101 cases, the model evaluates the right document through the wrong passage. A5 benefits from multiple retrieval views: among 266 relevant sources entering reranking, 233 have at least one support passage overlapping the gold span (87.6%), and the winning support overlaps in 217 cases (81.6%). This better support selection is consistent with A5 losing less parent-balanced Recall@10 than A4, although it still fails to outperform A3.

Tokenization creates a second bottleneck. Of 35,569 unique query--passage pairs scored by the Cross-Encoder, 34,669 (97.5%) exceed the 512-token limit and are truncated with a longest-first strategy. The longest pre-truncation pair contains 1,445 tokens. Among relevant winning passages, 289/300 (96.3%) in A4 and 259/266 (97.4%) in A5 are truncated. Therefore the experiment does not show that Cross-Encoders are unsuitable for plagiarism retrieval. It rejects the current combination of a generic mMARCO checkpoint, document support selection, 512-token truncation, and a hard top-50 gate.

### 5.5 Controlled Short-Input Follow-up on DEV1

A separate DEV1 comparison uses 30 parent annotations, 207 query windows and 200 source candidates, with no parent, suspicious-document or candidate-source overlap with DEV0. A6 retains A5's candidate pool, top-50 gate, support passage IDs, checkpoint and maximum pooling, but uses a token-bounded query prefix and a lexically selected support window. The fixed budgets are 192 query and 256 passage model tokens. Neither gold source spans nor TEST0 outcomes are used to select visible text.

| Method | Parent R@10 [95% CI] | Parent MRR@10 [95% CI] | Parent nDCG@10 [95% CI] | Parent R@100 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A5 | 0.7729 [0.6581, 0.8810] | 0.6293 [0.4908, 0.7664] | 0.6626 [0.5326, 0.7914] | 1.0000 |
| A6 | 0.7384 [0.6259, 0.8442] | 0.6270 [0.4953, 0.7573] | 0.6533 [0.5297, 0.7750] | 1.0000 |

The A6-minus-A5 difference in parent-balanced Recall@10 was -3.46 percentage points (paired 95% interval -10.28 to 3.69 points). The interval includes zero. This is a prespecified development comparison, not a confirmatory holdout result. Candidate Recall@100 is identical because only the top-50 head is reordered and the remaining tail is preserved.

Table 9 reports the paired comparison. Across scored support requests, A5 applies model truncation to 95.37% (19742/20700); A6 requires no additional model truncation (0/20700). A6 crops the query in 19700 requests and the passage in 19359, with token-boundary repair in 1498. These request counts include exact-pair cache reuse and are not unique-pair counts. Relative character ranges and hashes describe the scored text; original raw spans retain their candidate-provenance meaning.

The changed policy combines query-prefix trimming and lexical passage-window selection. The result therefore does not isolate the effect of truncation alone. The run is tied to commit `2a8c073072ba41af8f05c79e75124aeef833f0d3` and locked model revisions. Shared-model/cache timings are not independent cold-run latency. ## 6 Discussion

### 6.1 Answers to the Research Questions

**RQ1.** On DEV0, no single method dominates every retrieval objective. BM25 has the strongest point estimate under the primary parent-balanced early metric, Dense is strongest for query-window Recall@10 and parent-balanced Recall@100, and RRF provides the best query-window candidate Recall@100. The paired parent-bootstrap intervals for A2-A1 and A3-A2 cross zero at Recall@10, so DEV0 does not establish a reliable early-recall winner among BM25, Dense, and equal-weight RRF. The initial assumption that hybrid retrieval would automatically improve top-10 ranking is not supported under this configuration.

**RQ2.** On DEV0, the configured off-the-shelf Cross-Encoder does not improve the observed early-ranking point estimates, while candidate-depth recall is preserved. The A4-A2 bootstrap interval is wholly negative at Recall@10; the A5-A3 interval crosses zero. This is a negative development-set diagnostic for the present configuration, not a general comparison between bi-encoders and Cross-Encoders.

**RQ3.** Three observed failure modes are consistent with the losses: a hard top-50 gate that excludes sources demoted by fusion, support-passage selection that sometimes represents the correct document with an unrelated span, and near-universal input truncation. The rank-transition counts and span-overlap diagnostics turn an aggregate performance drop into concrete engineering hypotheses, but the DEV0 observational analysis does not isolate their causal effects. The controlled DEV1 follow-up removes additional model truncation through a combined query-prefix and lexical passage-window policy, yet its Recall@10 point estimate is lower than A5 and its paired interval includes zero. It neither establishes improvement nor isolates which input transformation caused the ranking changes.

**Auxiliary evidence-retrieval question.** On the 646 evaluable QASPER questions, E5 has higher point estimates than BM25 across the reported metrics. RRF slightly increases Hit@10 over E5, while E5 retrieves complete evidence sets more often and ranks the first relevant paragraph earlier. This trade-off supports reporting both partial and complete evidence retrieval for assessment-oriented systems rather than selecting a method from Hit@10 alone. The PAN and QASPER tasks use different query types, candidate units, gold definitions, and averaging schemes; their absolute metric values are not directly comparable. These findings do not evaluate whether the rubric verifier correctly interprets retrieved evidence.

### 6.2 Implications for the Next Evaluation

The DEV1 follow-up leaves DEV0 frozen and tests a shorter-input policy with the candidate pool, checkpoint, reranking depth, support count, and aggregation held fixed. Selection uses only inference-time information. Its result provides no basis to replace A5 with A6 as an improved method. Further input studies should separate query trimming from passage-window selection under a new protocol; candidate quotas, additional supports, and alternative pooling rules are distinct comparisons. Any final method decision must be frozen before a separately recorded TEST0 evaluation; TEST0 remains sealed in the present study. Negative/no-source queries and a suitable protocol are required before extending the claim to precision, false-positive rate, or operational screening quality.

For the full framework, Pipeline 1 needs independent evaluation of text alignment and similarity-type classification beyond the current exact-match application path. The QASPER comparison establishes a reproducible shared-retriever baseline, but Pipeline 2 still requires an approved report--rubric corpus, independent annotation and adjudication, and evaluation of retrieval and verification together. LOCAL-only baselines, LOCAL-versus-GRAPH ablation, evidence precision/recall, rubric-score agreement, abstention analysis, and instructor acceptance or correction rates remain necessary before making broader effectiveness claims. Public component benchmarks cannot establish end-to-end grading validity for Vietnamese student reports.

## 7 Limitations, Ethics, and Data Governance

The two PAN development samples are deliberately resource bounded: DEV0 has 30 parent annotations, 320 correlated windows, and 200 candidate documents; DEV1 has 30 different parent annotations, 207 windows, and a disjoint 200-document source pool. Neither is a full-corpus nor an official PAN result. Queries are oracle suspicious spans and all have a source, so the PAN experiment does not measure suspicious-span discovery, no-source rejection, false positives, text-alignment F1, similarity-type classification, document-level similarity percentage, or a plagiarism verdict. A1 and A2 confound retrieval model and chunking view. Parent-cluster bootstrap intervals account for dependence among windows from the same annotation, but they remain conditional on the small development samples and do not capture corpus-construction, model-selection, or annotation uncertainty. DEV0 was repeatedly inspected; DEV1 uses a prespecified policy comparison, but it is still development evidence. A6 combines query trimming and passage selection and does not isolate truncation alone. These PAN results cannot establish statistical superiority or replace an untouched holdout.

QASPER evaluates a different, paragraph-only task inside a known paper. Excluding 359 questions restricts the result to completely mapped, unambiguous paragraph evidence and removes figure/table, heading, and unanswerable cases; it does not establish performance on the whole dataset. Questions share papers, so the 646 question-macro observations are not asserted to be independent. No confidence interval, untouched holdout, or official QASPER F1 is reported. Tokenizer-level truncation of long E5 paragraphs was not audited, and gold evidence retrieval does not establish that the downstream verifier can interpret it correctly. The benchmark does not run authored checklist queries, the application's parser/chunker or LOCAL orchestration, rubric verification, scoring, or GRAPH routing. The auxiliary result therefore cannot establish end-to-end assessment quality.

No private student report contributes to the reported numbers. PAN is stored under its recorded research-use governance and retention rules. QASPER's official dataset card specifies CC BY 4.0; its revalidation manifest records an internal research diagnostic and no completed team pack-approval record. That administrative status is retained rather than replaced by a retrospective approval. Reporting the aggregate comparison in this manuscript does not close the project's assessment or dataset-acceptance gates. Raw corpus text and per-question predictions are excluded from the submitted artifacts. Future in-domain work must obtain documented authorization, remove names, student identifiers, supervisor names, and hidden document metadata, group related reports to prevent split leakage, and restrict access to approved researchers. Instructor review remains mandatory because a similarity match is not equivalent to misconduct and an automated rubric proposal can be wrong or incomplete.

## 8 Conclusion

We proposed an evidence-grounded architecture for similarity analysis and rubric-based assessment and evaluated source-retrieval components through PAN-PC-11 diagnostics and an auxiliary QASPER experiment. The DEV0 diagnostic compared BM25, multilingual Dense retrieval, RRF, and Cross-Encoder reranking. BM25 produced the highest parent-balanced Recall@10 point estimate, RRF increased query-window candidate coverage while changing parent-balanced early recall only slightly, and the current Cross-Encoder configurations reduced early-ranking point estimates. Parent-cluster intervals show that DEV0 cannot reliably separate the Recall@10 of BM25, Dense, and RRF; they provide a clearer negative diagnostic for the present Dense-plus-reranker configuration. Rank transitions, support overlap, and truncation reveal plausible failure mechanisms without establishing causality. A controlled follow-up on disjoint DEV1 data eliminates additional model truncation with bounded query/passage inputs, but does not improve Recall@10 over A5: the paired difference is -3.46 percentage points, with a 95% interval from -10.28 to +3.69. This does not establish equivalence or a general degradation.

The QASPER comparison adds human-annotated paragraph-level evidence retrieval on 646 evaluable DEV questions. RRF has the highest Hit@10 point estimate, while Dense has higher complete-evidence retrieval and MRR@10; the experiment does not establish a statistically reliable overall winner. It evaluates shared retrieval algorithms rather than rubric verification or scoring. Together, these component studies show why evidence retrieval must be evaluated at its actual task and unit of analysis. A frozen PAN holdout evaluation and a separately governed, independently annotated rubric/evidence study are still required before claiming end-to-end plagiarism detection or grading effectiveness.
